Behavioural Served because you finished a Strategy card

You have two minds. One of them is lazy.

System 1 is fast, intuitive and emotional. System 2 is slow, deliberate and effortful. Most choices run on System 1, which is why a confident snap judgment is so often a biased one.

Knowing which system is driving lets you catch your own errors before they cost you.

TThinking, Fast and Slow · Daniel Kahneman
Entrepreneurship

Stop perfecting. Start learning.

Build the smallest thing that tests your riskiest assumption, measure how real users react, then learn. The unit of progress is validated learning, not features shipped.

Most startups fail by building something nobody wants, beautifully.

LThe Lean Startup · Eric Ries
Operations

A system is only as fast as its slowest step.

Every process has one bottleneck that sets the pace of the whole. Improving anything that isn't the bottleneck is an illusion of progress. Find the constraint, exploit it, subordinate everything else to it.

Effort spent away from the constraint produces zero real throughput.

GThe Goal · Eliyahu Goldratt
Risk Served because you saved an Operations idea

The rare event isn't the exception. It's the story.

History is shaped by rare, unpredictable, high-impact events we rationalise only afterwards. We underestimate them because they are absent from the sample we learn from.

Build for a world where the unimaginable is routine, not for the average day.

BThe Black Swan · Nassim Taleb
Game theory

Think forward, reason backward.

Put yourself in the other player's shoes, anticipate their best response to your move, then work backward to your choice today. Your best action depends entirely on theirs.

Great strategists play the opponent's next three moves, not just their own.

AThe Art of Strategy · Dixit & Nalebuff
Innovation

Doing everything right is how great firms die.

Incumbents listen to their best customers and invest in sustaining better products, which is exactly why they miss the cheap, worse, disruptive technology that eats their market from below.

The discipline that made you win can blind you to what comes next.

IThe Innovator's Dilemma · Clayton Christensen
Economics

People respond to incentives, even ones you didn't intend.

Look past the moral story to the hidden incentive underneath and surprising behaviour starts to make sense. Data beats intuition when it reveals what people do, not what they say.

If behaviour looks irrational, you probably haven't found the real incentive yet.

FFreakonomics · Levitt & Dubner
Entrepreneurship Served because you linger on Entrepreneurship

Start with what you have, not what you'd need.

Expert founders don't begin with a goal and gather resources. They begin with who they are, what they know and whom they know, then let goals emerge. Control the future by making it, not predicting it.

You don't need the perfect plan. You need the next affordable step.

EEffectuation · Saras Sarasvathy
Tech & society Served because you saved a Gen AI idea

Progress isn't automatic. Someone chooses who it's for.

A thousand years of technology show the gains go to whoever steers it. New machines lift wages only when workers have the power to share in the productivity, otherwise they mostly enrich the people who own them.

The question for AI is not how powerful it gets, but who decides what it is used for.

PPower and Progress · Acemoglu & Johnson
Influence

Great work is not enough. Someone has to notice it.

Performance matters less to who gets ahead than we like to believe. Power comes from visibility, relationships and control of resources, and the people who get it ask for it, build it deliberately and tolerate conflict.

Waiting to be recognised is a strategy, and usually a losing one.

PPower · Jeffrey Pfeffer
Leadership Served because you saved an Influence idea

Idealism is a practice, not a pose.

Samantha Power went from war reporter in Bosnia to US Ambassador to the UN. Inside government she found that ideals survive only through compromise, persistence and small, concrete acts for the people in front of you.

You change institutions from the inside by staying in the room.

EThe Education of an Idealist · Samantha Power
History

Follow the money and you find the war.

A Marine general with two Medals of Honor argued in 1935 that war is fought by the many and profits the few. His fix: take the profit out, conscript capital before soldiers, and let the people who fight vote on whether to fight.

When someone gains from a decision, ask who pays for it.

WWar Is a Racket · Smedley D. Butler
Justice Served because you linger on Leadership

Each of us is more than the worst thing we've ever done.

Bryan Stevenson defended Walter McMillian, a Black man sentenced to death for a murder he didn't commit. The case shows how poverty and race shape who the justice system condemns, and what mercy asks of the rest of us.

Proximity to the people a system fails changes what you think it should do.

JJust Mercy · Bryan Stevenson
Markets

The story was worth more than the wine.

In 1985 a bottle of 1787 Lafite said to belong to Thomas Jefferson sold for $156,000. The provenance came from one collector and later collapsed under scrutiny. Experts, auction houses and buyers all wanted the story to be true.

Scarcity plus a great story disables due diligence. Check the provenance.

BThe Billionaire's Vinegar · Benjamin Wallace
Influence Served because you saved Pfeffer's Power

Whoever controls the money controls the city.

Robert Moses was never elected, yet he reshaped New York for forty years. Bridge tolls gave his public authorities money no mayor controlled, and with it came contracts, loyalty and the power to bulldoze whole neighbourhoods.

Real power often hides in unelected places: authorities, budgets and fine print.

PThe Power Broker · Robert A. Caro
Leadership

Pragmatism without principles loses its way.

Sergio Vieira de Mello spent 34 years at the UN, from Cambodia to running East Timor before independence. He would talk to anyone if it moved things forward, until he was killed in the 2003 bombing of the UN in Baghdad.

Legitimacy comes from the people you serve, not from your mandate.

CChasing the Flame · Samantha Power
